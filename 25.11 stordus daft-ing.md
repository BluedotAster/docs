Us humans have created innumerable ways to organize tools, workbenches and living spaces, long before the flat screen's time- Instead of reinventing one's methods for the comparatively new digital landscape, why not adapt the PC to the living world?
Stardust XR is the launchpad, bringing general computing into your ambient space from the fundamental level.
At its core, Stardust is a display server; the graphical system that projects app views in human-friendly form, and routes your inputs--keyboard, mouse, controllers, hand gestures and such--between apps and the wider operating system- It also can run concurrently with any other XR app as an OpenXR overlay. *Apps do not need to be specifically modified to use with Stardust.*
Stardust XR separates your PC's user interface from the conventions and constraints of physical hardware- Stardust's Client system organizes higher-level features into an expandable set of semi-independent units, each able to act selectively on different objects (or apps) within Stardust's environment. Your Stardust toolbox is not set in stone; unlike in other spatial-computing environments, Clients and other elements of Stardust's XR environment can be individually swapped out or expanded upon with no structurally-imposed limit, much like mods for games or virtual home furniture. *Nothing stops you from building the ultimate mobile battlestation, smart-room or functional dream-land from thin air.*
*The theory:*
Extended reality, spatial computing, techno-ontokinesis (or whatever one calls it these days) has one critical advantage as a medium: strict physicality is only a suggestion. A common 2D works for our current computing purposes, but a fixed-scale flat screen
Stardust proposes an alternate approach, albeit with another user-interface scheme to (attempt to) master: decoupling the form and function of a PC from static, physical hardware.
